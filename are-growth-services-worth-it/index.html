Are Instagram growth services worth it?

The cost maths against ads, buying followers, and doing it yourself.

Updated 25 October 2026 · Trustpilot figures read 25 October 2026 · Written by the Virallized team

For some accounts, on some services. The category ranges from 1.2 to 4.8 out of 5 on independent ratings, so the question is never “are they worth it” in general — it is which one, and whether your account is ready.

Two things determine the answer, and neither is the price. The first is whether the service delivers real people or accounts to a count. The second is whether your profile converts a visitor once they arrive.

The comparison that matters

  • Against Instagram ads. Ads stop the moment you stop paying and rent attention rather than build an audience. Most meaningful ad budgets start above the $99–$299 a month this category charges.
  • Against buying followers. Not comparable. Purchased followers damage the engagement ratio and leave within days.
  • Against doing it yourself. Entirely possible, and free. The cost is hours daily, which is what the service is actually selling.
  • Against doing nothing. If discovery is your constraint, nothing changes on its own.

When the spend is wasted

  • An empty or unclear profile. Outreach into a profile that does not explain itself converts nobody.
  • No defined niche. Targeting has nothing to aim at, and results follow.
  • Expecting content strategy. These services drive discovery. They do not make your content better.
  • Choosing on price alone. The cheapest tier in this category is almost entirely automated software, which carries the category's suspension complaints.

In a 200-account sample of Virallized client campaigns that had run at least 90 days, measured to 23 October 2026, the median account added 407 net followers a month and all 200 finished larger than they started. Net means after every unfollow and every account Instagram removed. Nothing was excluded, and four in five were on the Standard or Pro plan.

  • What a median means. Half the accounts added more than 407 a month and half added fewer. At the median pace, 1,000 new followers takes about two and a half months; that is arithmetic on the median, not a measured time.
  • Size changes the percentage. 407 a month is 20% of a 2,000-follower account and 2% of a 20,000-follower one.
  • Who measured it. Virallized, from its own campaign records, so it is not an independent audit. The Trustpilot record is the independent check, and your own account is the final one.
  • Check it on your own account. Instagram’s free insights show an account’s performance for any period in the last 90 days, so a first month can be measured on the account itself instead of on anyone’s sales page.

What Instagram, Meta and the FTC say

The rules below are quoted or summarised from the pages that publish them, each read on 25 October 2026.

  • Meta Transparency Centre, on what its spam policy, which covers Instagram, does not allow: “Attempting to or successfully selling, buying or exchanging for engagement, such as likes, shares, views, follows, clicks and use of specific hashtags.” Read it at source.
  • US Federal Trade Commission. The rule bans selling or buying fake indicators of social media influence, such as followers or views generated by a bot or hijacked account, where the buyer knew or should have known they were fake and used them to misrepresent their influence for a commercial purpose. It also bars a business from presenting a review site it controls as independent. Read it at source.
  • US Federal Trade Commission. The FTC's guidance says the rule took effect on 21 October 2024, and that followers who chose to follow an account are real people, not fake indicators. Read it at source.

Related questions

How much do Instagram growth services cost?

Roughly $19 to $299 a month across the category.

The $19–$64 tier is predominantly automated software. Managed human outreach starts around $99 and runs to $299 for the fastest tiers.

Do any of them actually work?

Ratings range from 1.2 to 4.9 out of 5, so the spread is enormous.

Three services in this directory hold ratings above 4.0 with one-star shares under 15%. Most of the rest sit below 3.0 with one-star shares above 40%.

What is a realistic return?

It depends on whether followers convert to something.

Reviewers of the better-rated services report leads, bookings, brand partnerships and direct sales. Those outcomes require an offer and a relevant audience, not just growth.

What is the safest way to try one?

A money-back guarantee, not a free trial.

Only three services in the category offer a refund at all, and one advertised guarantee turns out to be a trial extension. Check which you are being offered.

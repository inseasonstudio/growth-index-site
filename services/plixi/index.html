Why bot-follower complaints matter

When Plixi’s critical reviewers describe followers as bots or fake accounts, the cost goes beyond the count. Meta’s Inauthentic Behaviour policy says it removes fake accounts, so a count built on them falls when a clean-out reaches them. And Instagram’s guidance on recommendations lists accounts that repeatedly build a following through misleading practices, such as buying likes, among those it may not recommend.

How to check Plixi yourself

  • Before paying. Plixi’s Trustpilot profile carries a warning instead of a rating. Trustpilot places these when a business misuses the platform, for example by fabricating reviews.
  • On day one. Plixi’s published terms: “Guaranteed Results or It’s Free” on the Pro plan only. Note your follower count and the reach of your last few posts, then compare them later in Instagram’s free insights, which cover any period in the last 90 days.
  • After the first week. Open Account Status in Instagram’s settings. Instagram says it shows whether anything on the account goes against its rules and may lead to restrictions.
  • If spam accounts arrive. Instagram filters followers that are likely to be spam into a Potential spam list that can be cleared in one go (Instagram Help Centre).

What the notices mean

Trustpilot explains its warnings in its help article on consumer warnings. It places one on a business’s profile when the business misuses the platform, for example by fabricating reviews, and keeps it until the misuse stops and a reasonable time has passed.

A withheld rating and a falling count are different things. Path Social, Plixi and UpGrow carry the notice. Outfame, Ascend Viral and Virallized lost reviews in October without one, and all three still show a rating.

Trustpilot acts under its own guidelines, as its notice says. Fake reviews are also a legal matter: in the US, the Federal Trade Commission’s 2024 rule banning fake reviews and testimonials applies to the businesses that write or buy them.

Checking a growth service yourself

A Trustpilot count can change in a day, so check any service against your own account too. Instagram’s Help Centre covers each step.

  • Before paying. Open the service’s Trustpilot profile and look for a notice above the reviews. Then sort by most recent and read the one- and two-star reviews first.
  • On day one. Note your follower count and the reach of your last few posts, then compare them later in Instagram’s free insights, which cover any period in the last 90 days.
  • After the first week. Open Account Status in Instagram’s settings. Instagram says it shows whether anything on the account goes against its rules and may lead to restrictions.
  • If spam accounts arrive. Instagram filters followers that are likely to be spam into a Potential spam list that can be cleared in one go (Instagram Help Centre).

Instagram also lists the accounts it may not recommend to people who do not follow them. One kind it names: “Repeatedly engaged in misleading practices to build followings, such as purchasing ‘likes’.” (Recommendations on Instagram).
